\begin{abstract}
We built a pipeline that estimates the static leakage of CMOS logic in a 32\,nm low-power technology
(\VDDnom) under PVT variation from machine-learning models instead of full SPICE simulation. One
model per transistor case (NMOS/PMOS, ON/OFF) predicts all four terminal currents; it is trained on
\RowsPerCase{} ngspice simulations per case, oversampled at the bias points transistors actually
see inside gates, and after comparing four model types the selected model in all four cases is a
gradient-boosted tree ensemble (GBDT) with linear leaves. Gate leakage is composed from these models, with the channel current of every ON
transistor at near-zero $V_{DS}$ solved from Kirchhoff's current law, since predicting it directly
failed badly. The \TestCircuit{} is predicted as a sum of gate results plus a loading term for the
tunnelling current of driven inputs. Over 18 gate cases at \NumCorners{} PVT corners we reach a
mean accuracy of \GateMeanAcc{} (median error \GateMedianErr; \FreshMeanAcc{} on fresh corners),
and \CircuitMeanAcc{} on the test circuit over \CircuitNumSims{} vector--corner cases. A
per-transistor SPICE oracle shows the composition itself is exact (\OracleMeanAcc), so the
remaining gap to \qty{99}{\percent} is transistor-model error, mainly the source current of
stacked OFF PMOS and the PMOS gate-tunnelling current.
\end{abstract}
