\section{Error analysis}\label{sec:error}

Transistor errors are measured on validation rows, so the test set stays untouched; gate and
circuit errors on the full evaluation sets. All tables are generated by
\texttt{report/make\_analysis.py}.

\subsection{Transistor models}\label{sec:errtrans}
Table~\ref{tab:transvds} breaks the p95 error down by $|V_{DS}|$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ON $I_D$, $I_S$ near $V_D=V_S$.} The largest relative errors: the \texttt{nmos\_on}
        $I_D$ p95 is \TrVdsLowNmosOnId{} below \qty{1}{\milli\volt} against \TrVdsHighNmosOnId{}
        above \qty{0.3}{\volt}. The channel current crosses zero there, so a small absolute error
        is a large relative one. These rows dominate the ON statistics of
        Table~\ref{tab:chosen} but not the gate leakage, because their channel current is solved
        by KCL. The ON currents that do enter the leakage stay accurate at small $|V_{DS}|$:
        $I_G$ p95 \TrVdsLowNmosOnIg{} (\texttt{nmos\_on}) and \TrVdsLowPmosOnIg{}
        (\texttt{pmos\_on}), $I_B$ \TrVdsLowNmosOnIb{} and \TrVdsLowPmosOnIb.
  \item \textbf{OFF $I_S$.} Tiny and sign-changing where the source junction leakage balances
        the channel current; \BelowFloorNmosOffIs{} (\texttt{nmos\_off}) of rows lie below the
        floor. It is the hardest OFF current, and improves strongly with temperature
        (\texttt{nmos\_off} p95 \TrTempColdNmosOffIs{} below \qty{0}{\degreeCelsius},
        \TrTempHotNmosOffIs{} above \qty{80}{\degreeCelsius}) as the subthreshold current moves
        away from the floor.
  \item \textbf{OFF $I_B$} shows the opposite trend (\TrTempColdNmosOffIb{} cold,
        \TrTempHotNmosOffIb{} hot): at low temperature it sits on the constant junction floor; at
        high temperature the thermally generated junction current rises steeply.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Gates}\label{sec:errgates}
\paragraph{What the SPICE reference taught us.}
NAND2 \texttt{01} and INV \texttt{0} have the same leakage at every corner (largest deviation
\NandZeroOneInvDev): with $b=1$ the lower NMOS ties $x$ to ground, so the gate is electrically an
inverter. We used this as a wiring check of our decks. NOR2 shows the textbook stacking effect:
two OFF PMOS in series cut the leakage to \NorStackARatio{} of NOR2 \texttt{01} (median, lower in
\NorStackALower{} corners).

NAND2 \texttt{00}, however, leaks \emph{more} than NAND2 \texttt{01} (median ratio
\NandZeroARatio, lower in only \NandZeroALower{} corners). Its NMOS stack does reduce subthreshold
leakage, but both PMOS are now ON with gate at 0 and source at $V_{DD}$, giving two gate-tunnelling
paths instead of one. On this low-power card the threshold is high, so tunnelling is comparable to
subthreshold leakage and the extra path outweighs the stacking saving. Table~\ref{tab:nandtemp}
confirms it: subthreshold leakage grows steeply with temperature while tunnelling barely changes,
so the stacking effect reappears when hot. The same split explains the temperature trends: cases
with every PMOS OFF (\StrongCases) are pure subthreshold and rise by a factor
\StrongHotColdMin--\StrongHotColdMax{} from the coldest to the hottest corners; all others contain
a tunnelling ON PMOS and rise only \WeakHotColdMin--\WeakHotColdMax{} times.

The internal nodes behave as expected: in NAND2 \texttt{00}, $x$ floats up to
\VxZeroZeroMin--\VxZeroZeroMax{} until both OFF NMOS carry the same current; in NAND2 \texttt{10}
the ON upper NMOS passes a weak 1 and $x$ stops near \VxOneZeroFrac\,$V_{DD}$; in NOR2 \texttt{10}
the ON lower PMOS passes a weak 0 and $y$ stops near \VyOneZeroFrac\,$V_{DD}$. These intermediate
nodes are exactly the operating points that stack pinning targets.

\paragraph{Error against corner conditions and its sources.}
The gate error falls with temperature: median \GateColdMedianErr{} below \qty{0}{\degreeCelsius}
against \GateHotMedianErr{} above \qty{80}{\degreeCelsius}, with the bias dropping from
\GateColdBias{} to \GateHotBias{} (Figure~\ref{fig:errcorner}). Of all corner parameters the signed
error correlates most with \GateDriverFirst{} ($\rho=\GateDriverFirstRho$), then
\GateDriverSecond{} and \GateDriverThird. The oracle attribution (Table~\ref{tab:errorsources})
gives the mechanism: the largest single error source is \ErrorSourceFirst, the tunnelling current
of the ON PMOS, which depends exponentially on the PMOS oxide thickness and makes up the largest
share of the leakage when subthreshold conduction is weak, i.e.\ cold corners and high $|V_{th0}|$.
Hot corners are dominated by OFF transistors at the pinned full-$V_{DD}$ point, which the models
predict best.

\paragraph{Effects we ruled out.}
The AND2/OR2 gate-voltage snap is at most \GateSnapMax. In \ExtrapRows{} gate rows a transistor
input lies outside its training range (\ExtrapWhat, by at most \ExtrapMaxOver), and these rows are
no worse than the rest (median \ExtrapMedianErr{} vs.\ \NoExtrapMedianErr). The SPICE reference has
its own floor: the PMOS Kirchhoff residual of about $10^{-16}$\,A is \SpiceFloorRel{} of the
smallest gate leakage (\GateSpiceMin, \GateSpiceMinCase), so the smallest rows are only reliable to
a per cent or two.

\subsection{Test circuit}\label{sec:errcircuit}
Since the composition from SPICE gate values is exact (Section~\ref{sec:circuit}), the circuit
error is the gate ML error accumulated over \CircuitNumGates{} instances plus the loading term.
The part of that error that is common to a corner adds up instead of averaging out, which is
visible in temperature: the circuit bias is \CircuitColdBias{} below \qty{0}{\degreeCelsius} and
\CircuitHotBias{} above \qty{80}{\degreeCelsius}, and the median error falls from
\CircuitColdMedianErr{} to \CircuitHotMedianErr. At cold corners the systematic PMOS tunnelling
error dominates; at hot corners the remaining errors have mixed signs and cancel. Hence the signed
circuit error correlates with temperature (Spearman $\rho=\CircuitTempRho$) much more than with the
input vector.
